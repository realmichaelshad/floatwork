    How it works

    Marbling is printing from water. The pattern exists for a few minutes on the bath, and the silk takes it once.

    1. 1

      Float

      Ink is dropped onto a bath of water thickened with carrageenan, a seaweed extract. Each drop spreads into a ring and pushes the drops before it outward, so rings nest inside rings.

    2. 2

      Draw

      A stylus or a comb pulled through the surface drags the rings into veins, feathers and chevrons. The colors move without sinking, and they barely blend.

    3. 3

      Print

      Silk treated with alum is laid on the surface and lifted off with the whole pattern in it. The bath is skimmed clean afterwards, so no print can be made twice.

    The four inks

    • Indigo

      Indigo comes from the leaves of plants such as Indigofera tinctoria, and the blue only appears when the dissolved dye meets the air.

    • Madder

      Madder red comes from the root of the madder plant, and its colorant, alizarin, was the first natural dye to be made synthetically, in 1868.

    • Ochre

      Yellow ochre is clay colored by iron oxide, and people have painted with it for tens of thousands of years.

    • Lamp black

      Lamp black is soot gathered from burning oil, the same carbon that gives Chinese ink sticks their depth.

    Care

    • Hand wash in cool water with a mild soap, or dry clean.
    • Do not wring. Roll it in a towel and dry it flat, out of direct sun.
    • Iron on the reverse at a low silk setting.
    • Every dye fades in strong light over years, so store it folded in a drawer.
